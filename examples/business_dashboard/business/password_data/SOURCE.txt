Screening behavior and limits
-----------------------------
These public wordlists are not application credentials or recovery material.
The application loads them locally and never sends a prospective password or
its hash to an external service. It compares whole passwords case-insensitively,
ignoring surrounding whitespace for screening only; hashing and sign-in retain
the exact supplied characters. It also rejects exact account/service names.

Predictable-repeat screening is a local extension, not part of the upstream
corpora: a candidate is rejected when its entire normalized value consists of
two or more identical repetitions of a corpus entry. Only lengths dividing the
candidate's length are checked, through the shorter of half the candidate
length and the longest corpus entry (39 characters in this snapshot). This
adds at most 39 set lookups and does not reject unrelated phrases containing a
common word. It imposes no character-class composition rules.

The regression checks the fixed provenance digest of all 10,877 direct added
entries, their distinctness and allowed length, then submits every one to the
actual new-password policy. Its >=3,000 rejection assertion does not count
short passwords, repeated variants, or the implementation's set size.

This is a pinned historical snapshot, not a comprehensive or current breached-
password database. Passing screening does not establish password strength,
uniqueness, or a security-standard compliance level. Review upstream updates
explicitly, preserve both license notices, update the provenance and checksum
regressions, and rerun password and source-distribution checks.
